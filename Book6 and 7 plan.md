# Books 6 and 7 FIXED: Plan

A plan for a fan-written rewrite of the last two books, continuing from *Order of the Phoenix FIXED*. These books are a completely new story, so this plan doesn't track which beats come from the originals.

## Where Book 5 left things

- **Harry and Hermione:** Harry broke up with her on the last night of Book 5 "to protect her." She told him that was a stupid reason and made clear she isn't going anywhere.
- **The prophecy:** Harry knows that one of them must kill the other.
- **The Ministry:** Fudge saw Voldemort with his own eyes in the Atrium. Instead of admitting he's back, Fudge is covering it up.
- **Surveillance:** The Ministry started reading students' owl post during Umbridge's year.
- **Harry's prefect badge:** Dumbledore restored it at the end of Book 5.
- **Ron and Élodie:** They still write to each other. Élodie is home in France.

## What this rewrite leaves out

- **The Deathly Hallows:** They don't exist in this version.
- **The Horcrux hunt:** Horcruxes are only a side plot explaining why Voldemort can't be killed.
- **Camping:** Every location in Book 7 serves the war.
- **Snape loving Lily:** He has a new backstory.
- **Harry as a Horcrux:** The ending doesn't depend on Harry dying.

## Structure

Both books follow the familiar shape of the series:
1. **Privet Drive:** Harry is stuck with the Dursleys for the summer, cut off from the wizarding world. He learns how things are changing from the newspapers, which are full of lies, censorship, and things the Ministry can't quite hide.
2. **Hogwarts:** He goes back to school and sees those changes affect his everyday life, including his classes, meals, friends, and post.
3. **The year's crisis:** The school year builds to an ending that sets up the next book.

## The core idea: a fertilizer economy

### The chokepoint

Wizards can't conjure food from nothing, but they could eat Muggle food. So the chokepoint has to make Muggle food fail too.

- **The fertilizer:** Mooncalf dung, an established magical fertilizer, is refined into a compound that makes crops grow fast and survive blight. The refined compound is called **Moonmeal**.
- **The secret:** After Muggle food shortages during the Second World War, the Ministry secretly began supplying Moonmeal to British farms through a front company. Decades later, Britain's crops are bred to depend on it. Without Moonmeal, Muggle harvests fail too.
- **The fake gold:** Gringotts gold isn't real. It's goblin-made imitation gold that can be duplicated, but only by the goblins' secret machinery deep under Gringotts. Nobody outside the goblins knows.
- **What gives the Galleon value:** Since gold can be copied, a Galleon is really a promise that Gringotts will exchange it for Moonmeal. Gringotts holds Britain's Moonmeal reserves, and those reserves are the true backing of the currency.
- **Why that's dangerous:** Whoever controls Gringotts can print as much money as they want, but they can't print food. Money is unlimited, while Moonmeal is finite.
- **The source:** The largest mooncalf herds live in the Forbidden Forest and the hills of wizard Northern Ireland. Professors Sprout and Slughorn refine most of it in the Hogwarts greenhouses and potion cellars. Hogwarts's ancient protections are the only reason the Ministry hasn't seized it already.
- **Why Hogwarts food is so good:** The Hogwarts kitchens get first pick of the castle's own Moonmeal, which makes its food better than anywhere else in Britain. Students have noticed for six years without asking why.

This answers the Muggle food question and explains why both wizards and Muggles starve when Moonmeal is destroyed.

### Factions

| Side | Who | What they control |
|---|---|---|
| **The regime** | Fudge's Ministry, which works with Voldemort and is later run through Fudge under the Imperius Curse; Gringotts and the goblins | The government, the newspapers, the surveillance network, the currency, the gold-copying machinery, the Moonmeal reserves, and Ollivander's wand production |
| **The regime inside Hogwarts** | Ministry inspectors posted at the castle; Draco Malfoy, as chief negotiator; and the Slytherins loyal to their Death Eater families | Surveillance of the school, the Muggle-born records, and the threat of a civil war inside the castle |
| **The regime's allies** | The French Ministry, Durmstrang, and other European governments | Armies, which arrive for the final battle |
| **The resistance** | Hogwarts, including Slughorn and the Slytherins who follow him, and wizard Northern Ireland | The mooncalf herds, the refining greenhouses, and Northern Ireland's advanced magic |

### Fudge

At first, Voldemort doesn't control Fudge. Fudge chooses to work with him, because the truth would end his career. After the massacre, Voldemort places him under the Imperius Curse.

- **The cover-up:** He claims the battle at the Department of Mysteries was a break-in by reckless students led by Harry, and blames Sirius for the deaths. The Atrium sighting is called a trick staged by Dumbledore. Ministry witnesses are quietly Obliviated.
- **Doubling down, in secret:** He expands Umbridge's mail-reading program to the whole country, monitors Floo calls, and recruits portraits as informants. None of it is announced. Letters are opened and resealed perfectly, and the Prophet's stories are quietly rewritten before printing, so nothing looks censored. The only clues are the details that go missing.
- **The Quibbler:** It isn't banned. Its print runs keep mysteriously disappearing, and the Prophet reports that it's struggling financially.
- **The deal:** Voldemort lets Fudge stay Minister and keeps himself out of the headlines. In return, Fudge gives him the Ministry's records, including the Muggle-born registry, and helps bring Gringotts under his control.
- **Umbridge:** She returns to run the new Muggle-born Registration Office.
- **The inspectors:** He sends Ministry inspectors to Hogwarts "for student safety." They carry out the massacre in Book 6, and he protects them afterward.
- **The Imperius Curse:** After the massacre, Fudge panics and wants out of the deal. Voldemort puts him under the Imperius Curse. From then on, Fudge is the regime's most valuable asset: Voldemort can't appear in public, so he needs Fudge as the face of the government.
- **Why Fudge is vulnerable:** Several Order members hold Ministry positions with direct access to Fudge, including Kingsley Shacklebolt on his security detail and Arthur Weasley. Voldemort can't protect Fudge without revealing himself.
- **His fate:** The Order plans to assassinate him near the end of Book 6, but Voldemort kills Dumbledore first, and the plot is called off. In Book 7, once Voldemort no longer needs a public face, he kills Fudge himself.

### Why Northern Ireland

Wizard Northern Ireland has its own council, which never fully answered to the British Ministry. Its wizards are inventors and researchers rather than manufacturers, so they've developed magic Britain doesn't have, including long-range detection and the Vanishing Device. But they still buy British-made wands, since Britain is the manufacturing superpower. That dependence is what nearly destroys them.

## Rewritten backstories

### Snape

**The problem:** Snape's loyalty coming from his feelings for Harry's mother is a weak and strange motivation.

**The new backstory:**
- At school, Snape was secretly in love with **Mary Macdonald**, a Muggle-born Gryffindor in his year. Lily stays his oldest friend, but only a friend.
- His Death Eater friends attack Mary with dark magic in their sixth year. Snape says nothing. In Book 5, Harry sees Snape call Lily "Mudblood," and that cruelty now looks like part of a pattern.
- After he joins the Death Eaters, Voldemort learns about Mary and orders Snape to kill her as a loyalty test.
- **Snape does it.** He tells himself he has no choice, since refusing would mean both their deaths.
- His defection to Dumbledore is driven by that guilt. He isn't spying out of love. He's spying because he can never undo what he did.
- **Why Harry:** He protects Harry because Lily was the one friend who stood up for him and was killed by the man he served. It's a debt, not a romance.
- **Why Hermione matters:** In Book 5, Snape saw Harry's memories of Hermione during Occlumency. She reminds him of Mary, which is why he's so cruel to her and why he's finally willing to protect her.

**The payoff:** Halfway through Book 6, Voldemort orders the Ministry inspectors at Hogwarts to massacre the Muggle-born students. Snape knows about the plan and is expected to help. This time, he chooses differently and secretly gets Hermione and others out. Harry only learns the truth in Book 7.

### Dumbledore

**The problem:** In the original, Dumbledore's backstory with Ariana and Grindelwald explains his sadness but doesn't connect to the present story.

**The new backstory:**
- As young men, Dumbledore and Grindelwald planned to rule wizards and Muggles "for the greater good." Their plan was to control Muggles through food rather than force.
- Dumbledore was the brilliant one. He developed the method for refining mooncalf dung into Moonmeal and worked out how a hidden food supply could control a nation.
- Ariana, Dumbledore's sister, was attacked by Muggle boys as a child and never recovered. That attack was part of why he wanted Muggles controlled.
- Ariana died during a three-way duel between Dumbledore, Grindelwald, and their brother Aberforth. Nobody knows whose curse killed her.
- Dumbledore abandoned the plan and buried his research, but the Ministry later found it and built the Moonmeal economy from it.

**Why it matters now:** The weapon Voldemort is using is Dumbledore's invention. Dumbledore's guilt isn't just personal grief. It's responsibility for the system that will starve Britain. This also gives him a real reason to hide things from Harry.

**Grindelwald:** He's still imprisoned in Nurmengard, in continental Europe. He knows the full plan, which makes him a possible source of information as Europe turns against Hogwarts.

## Horcruxes: why Voldemort can't be killed

- In Book 6, Dumbledore discovers that Voldemort has split his soul into Horcruxes hidden across Britain. He and Harry learn that killing Voldemort is impossible while they exist.
- Dumbledore's hand is cursed while he destroys one of them, Marvolo Gaunt's ring.
- There's no Horcrux hunt. They're hidden in places the resistance can't reach, which helps explain why the war becomes about food, money, and weapons instead of killing one man.
- **The ending:** When wizard Northern Ireland vanishes the island of Great Britain, every remaining Horcrux disappears with it, along with Voldemort. The impossible task is solved by the war itself, not a scavenger hunt.

## Harry and Hermione's arc

1. **Apart (Book 6, opening):** Harry doesn't answer her letters at Privet Drive. At school, they're still prefects together, which makes it painful.
2. **Back together (Book 6, first third):** The surveillance state forces them to rely on each other, and Harry admits that pushing her away didn't make her safer.
3. **The massacre (Book 6, midpoint):** Hermione survives because Snape secretly gets her out. Harry thinks she's dead for several hours.
4. **Fighting the restraint (Book 6, second half):** They argue together against Dumbledore's decision not to strike back, then disagree with each other about the Order's plot to kill Fudge.
5. **Dumbledore's death (Book 6, end):** They stand together at the funeral, knowing that war is coming.
6. **Partnership in war (Book 7):** They help lead the resistance together. Hermione runs intelligence and strategy, while Harry is its public face and fighter.
7. **The final battle (Book 7):** The wand poison strikes, and they survive because Hermione worked out the danger in time.
8. **Northern Ireland (Book 7, end):** They escape with the survivors and start a new life.

## Book 6: The Quiet Takeover

*The detailed Book 6 plan, including the romance and Voldemort's attempt to capture Hermione, is now in Book 6 plan.md. This section is a shorter summary.*

**The book's question:** How long can Hogwarts keep from fighting back? Voldemort spends the whole book killing people without consequences, because Hogwarts is afraid of tearing itself apart. Only when he kills Dumbledore does Hogwarts finally strike back.

### Part 1: Privet Drive and the newspapers

- Harry spends the summer at Privet Drive, grieving Sirius and refusing to write back to anyone.
- **What the Prophet says:**
  - The Department of Mysteries was "vandalized by students under Dumbledore's influence," and Harry is named as the ringleader.
  - Sirius Black is blamed for the deaths that night.
  - Fudge announces the Muggle-born Registration Office under Umbridge, describing it as a harmless census.
  - The Quibbler stops arriving. The Prophet says it's struggling financially.
- **What Harry notices:** Nothing in the Prophet looks censored. The account of the Ministry battle is detailed and confident, but it leaves out everything Harry actually saw, including Voldemort in the Atrium, the Death Eaters' masks, and Fudge's own face when he arrived. Names of witnesses Harry remembers never appear. Harry was there, so he knows the story is missing pieces that only someone with power could have removed.
- **The Muggle news:** Vernon's newspaper reports a bridge collapse and strange deaths that the Muggle government can't explain. Harry knows what they are.
- **Hermione's letters:** They keep arriving even though he doesn't answer, and they look untouched. But she keeps referring to things that aren't there, like "as I said about Sirius" or "the article I sent you." The paragraph and the clipping are both missing. Harry can't prove anything, but he starts to suspect that someone is reading everything she writes to him.
- Dumbledore arrives to collect Harry and takes him to persuade Horace Slughorn out of retirement to teach Potions again.
- **Where the Ministry stands:** Fudge stays Minister. Death Eaters quietly take key Ministry jobs, including in Magical Law Enforcement, the Registration Office, and the department that oversees Gringotts.
- **Gringotts:** It quietly sides with Voldemort. The goblins are promised full control of Moonmeal production once Hogwarts falls. Their gold-copying machinery stays secret, and it isn't used to fund the regime yet.

### Part 2: Diagon Alley

- The Weasleys take Harry and Hermione shopping. Diagon Alley is quieter than usual, and several shops are closed. Their owners are Muggle-born, and nobody will say where they've gone.
- Ollivander's shop is boarded up. The Prophet says he has retired. Secretly, Voldemort has taken him to keep making wands.
- Fred and George's joke shop is booming. They sell defensive products, along with a new line of "privacy" items they won't explain. George mentions that their own post has been arriving oddly, and Harry remembers Hermione's missing paragraphs.
- Harry sees Draco Malfoy slip away to Knockturn Alley.
### Part 3: Back to Hogwarts

- **The welcome feast:** The food is as amazing as ever, and Ron declares that he'd fight a dragon for Hogwarts potatoes. Hermione wonders aloud why nothing anywhere else tastes like this.
- **How the changes affect school life:**
  - Every student's owl post is secretly opened before it's delivered. Letters look normal, but students notice gaps: a parent mentions news that never arrived, a sibling's letter skips a whole week, or a photo described in a letter isn't in the envelope.
  - Students have to register their blood status. Muggle-born students are marked in the school records, and some stop getting letters from home entirely.
  - The Prophet reads smoothly, but students whose families saw events firsthand notice what's missing. Attacks are reported without names, death tolls are lower than families say, and some stories vanish entirely. Older students start keeping handwritten records comparing the Prophet's version with what their families saw.
  - Portraits in the corridors have secretly been recruited as Ministry informants. At first, nobody knows. Students only gradually notice that the same painted figures always seem to be nearby when private things are discussed, and that Ministry inspectors know things they shouldn't.
  - Ministry inspectors are posted in the castle "for student safety." They sit in on classes, check registration papers in the corridors, and keep lists of Muggle-born students. Many are secretly Death Eaters.
- **The kitchens:** Hermione visits for S.P.E.W. and sees the house-elves cooking with sacks stamped with Professor Sprout's seal. The elves won't say what's inside, but they treat it as precious.
- **The mooncalves:** In a whimsical night scene, Hagrid takes the trio to see the Forbidden Forest's mooncalf herd dancing in the moonlight. He collects their dung before sunrise and explains that it's the most valuable thing in the forest. Harry thinks it's a joke.
- **Slughorn, the good Slytherin:**
  - He's vain, he collects famous students in his Slug Club, and he loves crystallized pineapple.
  - He secretly refines Moonmeal with Sprout. This is the real reason Dumbledore wanted him back.
  - Under the vanity, he's brave. He's the head of house that the loyal Slytherins follow, so he becomes the counterweight to Malfoy.
- Draco Malfoy has taken the Dark Mark. He's secretly repairing the Vanishing Cabinet in the Room of Requirement.
- Dumbledore begins private lessons with Harry about Voldemort's past. The lessons also cover Dumbledore's own past, though he holds back the part about Moonmeal.

### Part 4: Back together

- Harry and Hermione are still apart but patrol together as prefects.
- **Proving the surveillance:** They compare their summer letters and find the same gaps. Hermione tests it by writing a fake secret to her parents, and an inspector mentions it a week later. They also catch a portrait moving between frames to follow them. For the first time, they can prove that the Ministry is reading the mail and watching the castle.
- Hermione discovers that the Ministry is tracking every Muggle-born student. At the same time, Harry sees Ministry spies on the Marauder's Map inside the castle.
- They work together to protect the Muggle-born students, using their prefect rounds as cover.
- Harry admits that pushing her away didn't make her safer. They get back together.
- Because their letters are read and the portraits watch the corridors, they have to find private moments in places with no paintings. It's romantic and paranoid at the same time.

### Part 5: The massacre

- Voldemort orders the massacre to show that nowhere is safe for Muggle-borns, not even Hogwarts.
- The Ministry inspectors carry it out. One night, they announce a "registration check" and gather Muggle-born students using the lists they've kept all term. Many are killed.
- The inspectors were let into the school as Ministry officials, so the castle's protections didn't stop them. The surveillance system becomes the means of the massacre.
- Snape knew about the plan through the Death Eaters and was expected to help. Instead, he secretly gets Hermione and a group of other students out through a hidden passage.
- Harry believes Hermione is dead until Snape's group reaches the Room of Requirement. It's the worst night of his life.
- **The cover-up:** Fudge calls it the work of "a few rogue inspectors," promises an investigation, and blames Dumbledore's weak security. The investigation is run by Death Eaters, and the inspectors involved are quietly reassigned. The next morning's Prophet reports it calmly, with a much lower death toll and no names. Hogwarts students know exactly who's missing.

### Part 6: No consequences

- Fudge panics over the massacre and tries to back out of his deal with Voldemort. Voldemort places him under the Imperius Curse, so the Ministry is now under Voldemort's direct control.
- Hogwarts wants to strike back. The Order and the Northern Irish council propose cutting off Moonmeal.
- **Voldemort's dilemma:** He has considered killing Dumbledore, but Dumbledore is the main voice holding back the embargo. If Dumbledore dies, McGonagall and Northern Ireland will cut off Moonmeal at once. So Voldemort keeps Dumbledore alive and the negotiations going.
- **Malfoy as chief negotiator:** Voldemort sends Draco to negotiate with Dumbledore and McGonagall. Voldemort is holding Draco's parents, so Draco has to succeed or his family dies.
- **The civil war threat:** Draco's message is simple: if Hogwarts acts, the Slytherins loyal to their Death Eater families will rise up inside the castle, and Hogwarts will be fighting its own students.
- **Slughorn's counterweight:** He sits on Hogwarts's side of the table. He knows which Slytherins would follow Draco and which would follow him, and he isn't sure who would win.
- Dumbledore chooses restraint, because he fears a civil war inside the castle and knows an embargo would starve Muggles too. Draco wins concessions: the inspectors stay, the investigation is dropped, and the remaining Muggle-born students are "voluntarily" sent home.
- **How it changes school life:** Empty seats appear at every house table. The same inspectors still sit in on classes, and students have to walk past them every day. The DA starts meeting again in secret.
- **The pattern:** Voldemort keeps killing, including Muggle-born families, Order members, and a Ministry official who asks the wrong question. The Prophet reports each death as an accident or doesn't report it at all. Hogwarts never responds.
- **Harry and Hermione:** Hermione is furious that the school is trading Muggle-born safety for peace. Harry agrees with her and fights with Dumbledore about it. They're together now, so they argue against Dumbledore as a team.
- Harry sees that Draco is terrified, not proud. It doesn't make Harry forgive him, but it complicates his hatred.

### Part 7: The secrets

- Harry gets Slughorn's real memory about Voldemort asking how to make Horcruxes. Slughorn hands it over out of shame for teaching Tom Riddle, which is his first brave act.
- Harry learns about the Horcruxes and why Voldemort can't be killed. Dumbledore's cursed hand shows the cost of destroying even one.
- Hermione uncovers Gringotts's Moonmeal reserves, the Muggle farm contracts, and the gold-copying machinery. She realizes that the regime can print unlimited money but can't print food.
- Dumbledore finally tells Harry the truth: he invented Moonmeal.
- Harry is furious that Dumbledore hid something so important. It's the first time he sees Dumbledore as a man who made terrible mistakes.
- Dumbledore admits that his restraint has failed. But he still believes the negotiations are the only way to avoid a civil war inside the castle, so he keeps talking.

### Part 8: The plot against Fudge

- **The stalemate:** The negotiations have become a shield for Voldemort. As long as they continue, he can keep killing Muggle-borns with no response from Hogwarts.
- **The plan:** A faction of the Order led by Moody decides to assassinate Fudge, mainly to derail the negotiations between the Ministry and Hogwarts. If the Minister is killed, the talks can't continue, and Dumbledore can no longer hold Hogwarts back. The stalemate ends, and the embargo has to begin.
- **A secondary benefit:** Without Fudge, Voldemort also loses the public face of his government. For Moody, that's a bonus. The real target is the stalemate.
- **Why it can work:** Kingsley is on Fudge's security detail, and other Order members work in the Ministry and see Fudge every day. Voldemort can't stop them himself, because he can't show up at the Ministry without revealing that he controls it.
- They plan it without Dumbledore. They know he would forbid it, and they've stopped trusting his patience.
- Harry and Hermione find out by accident and disagree. Harry understands the Order's anger. Hermione warns that killing Fudge could make the Ministry's lies about Hogwarts look true. It's a serious argument, but they stay together.
- **The tip-off:** The plan is discussed near a portrait, one of the Ministry's informants. The surveillance system set up at the start of the book passes the warning straight to Voldemort.

### Part 9: Voldemort's calculation

- **His options:** Voldemort realizes that the stalemate is over either way.
  - If the Order kills Fudge, he loses the Minister he controls and the public face of his government.
  - If he kills Dumbledore, Hogwarts will start the embargo, but Fudge stays alive and in power.
- **His choice:** Killing Dumbledore is the better option. His death gives the Order an outlet, because the embargo becomes their response. That relieves the pressure to kill Fudge. Voldemort decides he can survive an embargo, but not losing the Ministry.
- Draco finishes repairing the Vanishing Cabinet, and his family's lives depend on it working.
- Voldemort and his Death Eaters invade Hogwarts through the Vanishing Cabinet at night. He has to get in and out before anyone outside Hogwarts can prove he was there.
- Voldemort kills Dumbledore on the Astronomy Tower while Harry watches, frozen and unable to help.
- **The plot ends:** With Dumbledore dead, the Order calls off the assassination and turns its anger into the embargo, just as Voldemort predicted.
- The Slytherin uprising begins. Slughorn leads the loyal Slytherins to stand with McGonagall. It's his finest moment, and it proves that the civil war threat was weaker than everyone feared.
- Draco flees with the Death Eaters, but doesn't fight anyone. Harry notices.
- **The embargo:** McGonagall becomes acting headmistress. She and the Northern Irish council stop all Moonmeal shipments. Hogwarts declares itself independent of the Ministry.
- **The Prophet's version:** Fudge, under the Imperius Curse, claims that Dumbledore was killed by his own reckless followers. He also reveals the Order's plot against him, calling it a plan by "Dumbledore's terrorists." Harry saw Voldemort on the tower, but nobody prints his account.
- Dumbledore's funeral is held by the lake. Harry and Hermione stand together, knowing that the next year will be a war.

## Book 7: The Fertilizer War

**The book's question:** Can Hogwarts survive a war against an enemy that can print unlimited money while food runs out?

### Part 1: Privet Drive and the newspapers

- Harry spends one last summer at Privet Drive. His mother's protection ends when he turns 17, so the Order plans to move him to Hogwarts before his birthday.
- **What the Prophet says:**
  - Hogwarts is called a "rogue institution," and its Moonmeal embargo is blamed for rising food prices.
  - Harry is named an enemy of the state, and the Order's plot against Fudge is used as proof that Hogwarts is run by terrorists.
  - Fudge announces emergency powers. Gringotts announces "temporary" limits on withdrawals.
- **What the Muggle news says:** Vernon's newspaper reports failing harvests and empty supermarket shelves, and the Muggle government blames a mysterious crop blight. Vernon is outraged by the price of bacon. Harry realizes the wizarding war is starving people who've never heard of Moonmeal.
- **The Dursleys:** They're furious, frightened, and finally willing to leave. The Order takes them into hiding.
- **Hermione's parents:** Hermione alters their memories and sends them to Australia so they'll be safe. This is what saves them from the ending.
- **The escape:** Thestral squadrons and war brooms carry Harry out of Privet Drive under Death Eater attack. It's the first battle of the war.

### Part 2: Hogwarts at war

- **How the war changes school life:**
  - Hogwarts is now a fortress and refugee camp. Muggle-born families sleep in classrooms, and the Room of Requirement becomes a field hospital.
  - The Great Hall feast is rationed. For the first time, Hogwarts food is ordinary, then scarce. Ron takes it personally.
  - Classes continue, but seventh-years train in combat between lessons. The DA becomes the resistance's officer corps.
  - No owls from outside can be trusted. Students learn about the world through Northern Irish broadcasts and smuggled Muggle newspapers.
  - Galleons are almost worthless inside the castle, so students trade favors, rations, and Fred and George's products instead.
- Harry and Hermione become leaders of the student resistance. Hermione runs intelligence and strategy, while Harry is its public face and fighter.

### Part 3: The embargo

- Without new Moonmeal, Gringotts starts using up its reserves to keep the Galleon and the food supply going.
- The regime starts using the goblin machinery to copy gold and pay its armies and allies. This is the first time the money printing affects ordinary people. With more and more money chasing less and less Moonmeal, prices soar and people start to panic.
- The Prophet blames Hogwarts and Harry for the shortages. Some families with students at Hogwarts start turning against the school.

### Part 4: The shadow war

Both sides fight a covert war with magical weapons.

- **The regime's weapons:**
  - **Hunter Bludgers:** Enchanted to chase a single target.
  - **War owls:** Owls carrying cursed packages, which can find anyone.
  - **Floo strikes:** Curses sent through fireplaces.
  - **Goblin-silver warheads:** Goblin-made metal that stores a Killing Curse or Fiendfyre, then releases it on impact.
- **The resistance's weapons:**
  - **War brooms:** Ridden in pairs, with a pilot and a spell-gunner.
  - **Thestral squadrons:** Mostly invisible, except to people who have seen death.
  - **Fred and George's decoys:** Fireworks that confuse Hunter Bludgers.
- **Raids:** Resistance teams leave Hogwarts to attack Moonmeal convoys and Gringotts storage vaults, then return. Every mission has a purpose.
- **Snape revealed:** Harry learns about Mary Macdonald and that Snape saved Hermione during the massacre.
- **Fudge's end:** Voldemort no longer needs a public face, so he kills Fudge and rules openly.

### Part 5: The reserves run down

- Gringotts runs low on Moonmeal. Muggle harvests fail across Britain.
- **The wand poison:** Hermione notices a strange trace in a dead Death Eater's new wand. She works out that Ollivander is being forced to plant a hidden poison in every new wand, set to be triggered remotely.
- Most young witches and wizards in Britain carry Ollivander wands, including most Hogwarts students. Wizard Northern Ireland buys British wands too.
- The resistance has only partial protection. Students switch to old family wands or wands made elsewhere, but there aren't enough to go around.

### Part 6: The final battle

- Voldemort, Gringotts, and the Ministry call in their European allies, including France and Durmstrang. A massive army marches on Hogwarts.
- **The poison:** The regime triggers it, and many Hogwarts defenders die. Those who switched wands because of Hermione's warning survive.
- **Hogwarts's weapon:** The founders' wards around the castle are turned outward and detonated, destroying the enemy army. The blast also destroys the Moonmeal greenhouses and Gringotts's last reserves.
- **Collapse:** The Galleon becomes worthless. Without Moonmeal, the next harvest fails, and both wizards and Muggles across Britain start to starve.
- **Small character beats:**
  - Élodie's letters warn Ron that France is mobilizing.
  - Krum refuses to fight against Hogwarts.
  - Draco switches sides, or at least refuses to fight, depending on how his arc develops.

### Part 7: Northern Ireland and the Vanishing

- The surviving resistance escapes to wizard Northern Ireland.
- Voldemort, now ruling a collapsed Britain, prepares to trigger the wand poison in Northern Ireland.
- Northern Ireland's advanced detection magic learns about the plan in time.
- The Northern Irish council uses the **Vanishing Device**, a national-scale Vanishing Spell, to make the island of Great Britain disappear.
- Voldemort and every remaining Horcrux vanish with it.

### Epilogue

- Harry and Hermione build a new life in Northern Ireland and are happy together.
- Ron is with them, and Élodie finally joins him.
- The Weasleys who survived rebuild nearby.

- **Harry's role in the ending:** The prophecy says one must kill the other, but the Vanishing does it instead. Decide whether the prophecy was false, misunderstood, or fulfilled indirectly.
- **Hermione's parents:** Sending them to Australia is what saves them from the Vanishing.
- **The Dursleys:** They're taken into hiding in Book 7. Decide where they are when Great Britain vanishes.
- **Ollivander:** His disappearance in Book 6 sets up the wand poison.
- **Northern Ireland:** It needs small setups in Book 6, such as Moonmeal shipments, a visiting council member, or Hermione's research.
- **Fake gold:** Keep it a total secret until Hermione's discovery near the end of Book 6. The regime doesn't print money or cause inflation until Book 7.
- **Censorship:** In Book 6, it's invisible. Letters look untouched and the Prophet reads smoothly, so readers only notice what's missing. In Book 7, after Dumbledore's death, the propaganda becomes open.
- **Hogwarts food:** Moonmeal can be hinted at with a line about the food in earlier books, if those outlines are revised.
- **Slughorn:** He's the leader of the loyal Slytherins and a Moonmeal refiner, as well as the source of the Horcrux memory.
- **Draco:** He's coerced, not proud, and never fights during the uprising. That leaves room for him to change sides in Book 7.
- **Dumbledore's death:** Voldemort kills him instead of Snape, so Snape's cover in Book 7 comes from his role in the massacre.
- **The massacre:** Ministry inspectors carry it out, not Death Eaters through the Vanishing Cabinet. The Cabinet is used only once, when Voldemort invades at the end of Book 6.
- **The assassination plot:** The Order plans it without Dumbledore's knowledge, but it never happens. The portraits leak it to Voldemort, which pays off the surveillance setup from the start of Book 6.
- **Dumbledore's death:** It's a preemptive strike to keep Fudge alive. Voldemort trades an embargo he thinks he can survive for keeping control of the Ministry.
- **Fudge:** He works with Voldemort willingly in the first half of Book 6, then is put under the Imperius Curse after the massacre. His cover-up of Book 5 is still his own choice, made to preserve his power.
- **Voldemort's limits:** He can't appear at the Ministry without revealing that he controls it. That's why the Order can threaten Fudge and why the Hogwarts invasion has to be quick and secret.

## Open decisions

1. **Snape and Mary:** I'd have Snape kill Mary on Voldemort's order, because it makes his loyalty about guilt and his choice to save Hermione more meaningful. The alternative is that she's killed because of information he gave, which is softer but still his fault.
2. **The prophecy:** I'd say it was fulfilled indirectly. Harry is the one who learns about the wand poison's trigger in Northern Ireland and warns the council, so his choice leads to Voldemort's end.
3. **Britain's Muggles:** Vanishing Great Britain would kill or erase millions of Muggles and leave the survivors' happy ending feeling very different. I'd keep the Vanishing, but have the epilogue acknowledge the cost: Harry and Hermione are happy, but they also live with what was lost and help lead the rebuilding effort. An alternative is that the Vanishing sends Britain into another state of being rather than destroying it, leaving room to bring it back.
4. **Who decides to use the founders' wards:** I'd make it McGonagall's decision, so Harry isn't responsible for the starvation, but he has to live with it.
5. **Harry and the assassination plot:** I'd keep Harry and Hermione out of it, with Harry sympathizing and Hermione opposing it. The alternative is that Harry joins the plot, which would make Dumbledore's death partly his fault and give him much heavier guilt in Book 7.
6. **Whether Dumbledore knew:** I'd have Dumbledore find out about the plot just before he dies and understand what Voldemort is doing. He chooses not to run, because he knows his death will finally free Hogwarts to act. This makes his death partly a sacrifice. The alternative is that he never knows, which is more tragic.
