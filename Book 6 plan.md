# Book 6 FIXED: Plan

A detailed plan for Book 6 of the fan-written rewrite, continuing from *Order of the Phoenix FIXED*. The shared worldbuilding for Books 6 and 7, including Moonmeal, the fake gold, the factions, and Snape's and Dumbledore's backstories, is in *Book6 and 7 plan.md*.

**The book's question:** How long can Hogwarts keep from fighting back? Voldemort spends the whole book killing people without consequences, because Hogwarts is afraid of tearing itself apart. Only when he kills Dumbledore does Hogwarts finally strike back.

**The romance question:** Book 5 ended with Harry pushing Hermione away to protect her. Book 6 asks whether he can learn to stand beside her instead, even after Voldemort targets her directly.

## World recap

- **Moonmeal:** A fertilizer refined from mooncalf dung. Wizard and Muggle farms in Britain depend on it, and it's the real backing of the Galleon. Hogwarts and wizard Northern Ireland produce most of it.
- **Gringotts:** Its gold is fake and can be copied by secret goblin machinery. The machinery isn't used to fund the regime until Book 7.
- **Fudge:** He covers up Book 5 and works with Voldemort to stay in power. After the massacre, Voldemort puts him under the Imperius Curse.
- **Surveillance:** Mail reading, Floo monitoring, and informant portraits are all secret. The only clues are the details that go missing.
- **Ministry inspectors:** They're posted at Hogwarts "for student safety," and many are secretly Death Eaters.

## Harry and Hermione's romance

### The arc

1. **Silence (Part 1):** Harry doesn't answer her letters, but he reads them over and over. The missing paragraphs are the parts where she tries to comfort him about Sirius.
2. **Awkward (Part 2):** Their first meeting since the breakup is in Diagon Alley, in front of a Muggle-born family's closed shop.
3. **Partners again (Part 3):** Prefect rounds force them to work side by side, and they gradually fall back into being a team.
4. **Back together (Part 4):** Proving the surveillance together leads to an honest apology in the one room without portraits.
5. **Secret couple (Part 4):** They're together, but every letter and corridor is watched, so they have to invent their own ways to be private.
6. **Public (Part 5):** At Slughorn's Christmas party, Harry publicly stands up for her, and they stop hiding.
7. **The capture attempt (Part 6):** Voldemort tries to take her during the massacre. Harry thinks she's dead.
8. **The test (Part 7):** Harry's old instinct is to push her away to keep her safe. This time, he doesn't, and they promise to tell each other everything.
9. **Disagreement (Part 8):** The Order's plot against Fudge gives them their hardest argument, and they get through it because of that promise.
10. **Together for the war (Part 9):** At Dumbledore's funeral, Harry chooses to face the war beside her.

### Romance beats

- **Their own private language:** Hermione invents a charm that hides real messages inside ordinary homework notes. They also use small signals on prefect rounds, like a touch on the arm meaning "portrait nearby." Ron finds it nauseating and is secretly pleased for them.
- **The Room of Requirement:** When they need a place with no paintings, it gives them a small room with a fireplace and two armchairs. It becomes their room for the rest of the book. Once, they hear something heavy being moved behind a wall, which is Draco's Vanishing Cabinet.
- **Slughorn's Christmas party:** Harry brings Hermione. A Ministry guest snubs her for being Muggle-born, and Harry calls him out in front of the whole party. Slughorn is delighted by the drama. It's the first time they're openly a couple since Rita's article in Book 4.
- **Christmas at the Burrow:** Hermione spends part of the holiday there. Mrs. Weasley is thrilled. Harry gives Hermione something small and personal, not expensive, and she cries. It's the last peaceful moment before the massacre.
- **Her parents:** Hermione is afraid the Muggle-born registry will lead Death Eaters to her family. Harry is the first person she tells, and they start planning how to protect them. This sets up her decision to send them to Australia in Book 7.
- **"I love you":** Harry says it first, on the night he thought she was dead. She says it back.
- **Her line after the massacre:** When Harry blames himself for her being targeted, she tells him that he doesn't get to make her danger about him. She's in danger for being Muggle-born, and she'll fight it as herself, not as his girlfriend. This directly answers his mistake in Book 5.

## Why Voldemort targets Hermione

### Why he hasn't taken her before

Voldemort has wanted to use Hermione against Harry since Book 5, when he learned through their mind link how much she means to Harry. But openly capturing Harry Potter's girlfriend would be an act of war against Hogwarts. It would end the negotiations that let him keep killing without consequences, and could trigger the Moonmeal embargo.

The massacre is his chance. Dozens of Muggle-born students are being taken in the dark, so one more disappearance would be blamed on the "rogue inspectors" and covered up with everything else.

### Why he wants her alive

Hermione is worth more to Voldemort as a hostage than dead. With her in his hands, he could control Harry: force him to stay silent, draw him into a trap, or make him surrender.

### How it fails

- The inspectors have orders to take Hermione alive and bring her out of the castle.
- Snape knows about the order through the Death Eaters. He intercepts the inspectors in a hidden passage, takes Hermione from them, and leads her and other Muggle-born students to safety.
- Hermione doesn't know why Snape saved her. Harry later learns about the capture order from Dumbledore, who learned it from Snape without revealing his source.

### Why this works

- It keeps Snape's rescue as the payoff of his backstory, because he saves a Muggle-born girl when he once killed Mary.
- It makes the massacre personal without turning Book 6 into a rescue mission.
- It gives the second half of the book a lasting threat: Voldemort could try again at any time, which tests whether Harry will push her away.

## Part 1: Privet Drive and the newspapers

- Harry spends the summer at Privet Drive, grieving Sirius and refusing to write back to anyone.
- **What the Prophet says:**
  - The Department of Mysteries was "vandalized by students under Dumbledore's influence," and Harry is named as the ringleader.
  - Sirius Black is blamed for the deaths that night.
  - Fudge announces the Muggle-born Registration Office under Umbridge, describing it as a harmless census.
  - The Quibbler stops arriving. The Prophet says it's struggling financially.
- **What Harry notices:** Nothing in the Prophet looks censored. The account of the Ministry battle is detailed and confident, but it leaves out everything Harry actually saw, including Voldemort in the Atrium, the Death Eaters' masks, and Fudge's own face when he arrived. Names of witnesses Harry remembers never appear.
- **The Muggle news:** Vernon's newspaper reports a bridge collapse and strange deaths that the Muggle government can't explain. Harry knows what they are.
- **Hermione's letters:** They keep arriving even though he doesn't answer, and they look untouched. But she keeps referring to things that aren't there, like "as I said about Sirius" or "the article I sent you." Harry can't prove anything, but he starts to suspect someone is reading everything she writes to him.
- **The romance:** Harry rereads her letters every night. He wants to answer and can't find the words. The missing paragraphs hurt most, because they're where she was trying to comfort him.
- Dumbledore arrives to collect Harry and takes him to persuade Horace Slughorn out of retirement to teach Potions again.
- **Where the Ministry stands:** Death Eaters quietly take key Ministry jobs, including in Magical Law Enforcement, the Registration Office, and the department that oversees Gringotts.
- **Gringotts:** It quietly sides with Voldemort. The goblins are promised full control of Moonmeal production once Hogwarts falls. Their gold-copying machinery stays secret.

## Part 2: Diagon Alley

- The Weasleys take Harry and Hermione shopping. Diagon Alley is quieter than usual, and several shops are closed. Their owners are Muggle-born, and nobody will say where they've gone.
- **The romance:** Harry and Hermione's first meeting since the breakup happens outside one of the closed shops. It belonged to a Muggle-born family Hermione bought her first quills from. She's upset, and Harry stands beside her without knowing what to say. It's awkward, but it's a start.
- Ollivander's shop is boarded up. The Prophet says he has retired. Secretly, Voldemort has taken him to keep making wands.
- Fred and George's joke shop is booming. They sell defensive products, along with a new line of "privacy" items they won't explain. George mentions that their post has been arriving oddly, and Harry remembers Hermione's missing paragraphs.
- Harry sees Draco Malfoy slip away to Knockturn Alley.

## Part 3: Back to Hogwarts

- **The welcome feast:** The food is as amazing as ever, and Ron declares that he'd fight a dragon for Hogwarts potatoes. Hermione wonders aloud why nothing anywhere else tastes like this.
- **How the changes affect school life:**
  - Every student's owl post is secretly opened before it's delivered. Letters look normal, but students notice gaps: a parent mentions news that never arrived, a sibling's letter skips a whole week, or a photo described in a letter isn't in the envelope.
  - Students have to register their blood status. Muggle-born students are marked in the school records, and some stop getting letters from home entirely.
  - The Prophet reads smoothly, but students whose families saw events firsthand notice what's missing. Older students start keeping handwritten records comparing the Prophet's version with what their families saw.
  - Portraits in the corridors have secretly been recruited as Ministry informants. At first, nobody knows.
  - Ministry inspectors are posted in the castle "for student safety." They sit in on classes, check registration papers in the corridors, and keep lists of Muggle-born students.
- **The kitchens:** Hermione visits for S.P.E.W. and sees the house-elves cooking with sacks stamped with Professor Sprout's seal. The elves won't say what's inside, but they treat it as precious.
- **The mooncalves:** In a whimsical night scene, Hagrid takes the trio to see the Forbidden Forest's mooncalf herd dancing in the moonlight. He collects their dung before sunrise and explains that it's the most valuable thing in the forest. Harry thinks it's a joke. Watching the dance, he catches Hermione smiling and forgets to look at the mooncalves.
- **Slughorn, the good Slytherin:**
  - He's vain, he collects famous students in his Slug Club, and he loves crystallized pineapple.
  - He secretly refines Moonmeal with Sprout. This is the real reason Dumbledore wanted him back.
  - Under the vanity, he's brave. He's the head of house that the loyal Slytherins follow, so he becomes the counterweight to Malfoy.
- **Prefect rounds:** Harry and Hermione patrol together every evening. At first they talk only about duties. Gradually, they start talking about everything else.
- Draco Malfoy has taken the Dark Mark. He's secretly repairing the Vanishing Cabinet in the Room of Requirement.
- Dumbledore begins private lessons with Harry about Voldemort's past. The lessons also cover Dumbledore's own past, though he holds back the part about Moonmeal.

## Part 4: Back together

- **Proving the surveillance:** Harry and Hermione compare their summer letters and find the same gaps. Hermione tests it by writing a fake secret to her parents, and an inspector mentions it a week later. They also catch a portrait moving between frames to follow them. For the first time, they can prove the Ministry is reading the mail and watching the castle.
- Hermione discovers that the Ministry is tracking every Muggle-born student. At the same time, Harry sees Ministry spies on the Marauder's Map inside the castle.
- They work together to protect the Muggle-born students, using their prefect rounds as cover.
- **The reunion:** They need a place where nobody can listen, so they ask the Room of Requirement for a room without paintings. There, Harry finally apologizes. He admits that pushing her away didn't make her safer. It only left her alone. They get back together.
- **A secret couple:** Because of the surveillance, they keep it quiet. Hermione's hidden-message charm and their signals on rounds become part of daily life. Ron is the only one who knows, and he keeps watch for portraits.
- **Her parents:** Hermione confides that she's afraid the registry will lead Death Eaters to her family. Harry promises to help her protect them.

## Part 5: Christmas

- **Slughorn's Christmas party:** Harry brings Hermione as his date. A Ministry guest snubs her for being Muggle-born, and Harry calls him out in front of everyone. Slughorn is delighted by the drama and later quietly tells Hermione that she's one of the finest students he has ever taught. Harry and Hermione stop hiding the relationship.
- **Christmas at the Burrow:** Hermione spends part of the holiday with the Weasleys. Mrs. Weasley is thrilled. Harry gives her a small, personal present, and she cries. It's the last peaceful moment before the massacre.
- Ron writes to Élodie over the holidays and gets a reply full of French news that doesn't match what the Prophet says. It's a small hint that the censorship reaches abroad too.

## Part 6: The massacre

- Voldemort orders the massacre to show that nowhere is safe for Muggle-borns, not even Hogwarts.
- The Ministry inspectors carry it out. One night, they announce a "registration check" and gather Muggle-born students using the lists they've kept all term. Many are killed.
- The inspectors were let into the school as Ministry officials, so the castle's protections didn't stop them. The surveillance system becomes the means of the massacre.
- **The capture attempt:** The inspectors have a separate order to take Hermione alive and bring her to Voldemort as a hostage against Harry.
- **Snape's choice:** Snape knew about both orders and was expected to help. Instead, he intercepts the inspectors in a hidden passage, takes Hermione from them, and leads her and a group of other students to safety.
- **Harry's worst night:** Hermione is listed as missing, presumed dead. Harry searches the castle for hours. He finds her when Snape's group reaches the Room of Requirement, in the room they made for themselves.
- **"I love you":** That night, Harry says it first. Hermione says it back.
- **The cover-up:** Fudge calls it the work of "a few rogue inspectors," promises an investigation, and blames Dumbledore's weak security. The investigation is run by Death Eaters, and the inspectors involved are quietly reassigned. The next morning's Prophet reports it calmly, with a much lower death toll and no names. Hogwarts students know exactly who's missing.

## Part 7: No consequences

- Fudge panics over the massacre and tries to back out of his deal with Voldemort. Voldemort places him under the Imperius Curse, so the Ministry is now under Voldemort's direct control.
- Hogwarts wants to strike back. The Order and the Northern Irish council propose cutting off Moonmeal.
- **Voldemort's dilemma:** He has considered killing Dumbledore, but Dumbledore is the main voice holding back the embargo. If Dumbledore dies, McGonagall and Northern Ireland will cut off Moonmeal at once. So Voldemort keeps Dumbledore alive and the negotiations going.
- **Malfoy as chief negotiator:** Voldemort sends Draco to negotiate with Dumbledore and McGonagall. Voldemort is holding Draco's parents, so Draco has to succeed or his family dies.
- **The civil war threat:** Draco's message is simple: if Hogwarts acts, the Slytherins loyal to their Death Eater families will rise up inside the castle, and Hogwarts will be fighting its own students.
- **Slughorn's counterweight:** He sits on Hogwarts's side of the table. He knows which Slytherins would follow Draco and which would follow him, and he isn't sure who would win.
- Dumbledore chooses restraint, because he fears a civil war inside the castle and knows an embargo would starve Muggles too. Draco wins concessions: the inspectors stay, the investigation is dropped, and the remaining Muggle-born students are "voluntarily" sent home.
- **Hermione stays:** She refuses to leave. As a prefect and a target, she says leaving would mean Voldemort had won. Harry backs her.
- **How it changes school life:** Empty seats appear at every house table. The same inspectors still sit in on classes, and students have to walk past them every day. The DA starts meeting again in secret.
- **The pattern:** Voldemort keeps killing, including Muggle-born families, Order members, and a Ministry official who asks the wrong question. The Prophet reports each death as an accident or doesn't report it at all. Hogwarts never responds.
- **The romance test:**
  - Dumbledore tells Harry that Voldemort wanted Hermione alive as a hostage against him and could try again.
  - Harry's instinct is to push her away again. He almost does. Then he tells her everything instead.
  - Hermione tells him that he doesn't get to make her danger about him. She's in danger for being Muggle-born, and she'll fight it as herself.
  - They promise to tell each other everything from now on. It's a direct answer to the mistake that ended Book 5.
- **Fighting the restraint:** Hermione is furious that the school is trading Muggle-born safety for peace. Harry agrees with her and fights with Dumbledore about it. They argue against him as a team.
- Harry sees that Draco is terrified, not proud. It doesn't make Harry forgive him, but it complicates his hatred.

## Part 8: The secrets and the plot against Fudge

- Harry gets Slughorn's real memory about Voldemort asking how to make Horcruxes. Slughorn hands it over out of shame for teaching Tom Riddle, which is his first brave act.
- Harry learns about the Horcruxes and why Voldemort can't be killed. Dumbledore's cursed hand shows the cost of destroying even one.
- Hermione uncovers Gringotts's Moonmeal reserves, the Muggle farm contracts, and the gold-copying machinery. She realizes the regime could print unlimited money, but can't print food.
- Dumbledore finally tells Harry the truth: he invented Moonmeal. Harry is furious that Dumbledore hid something so important. It's the first time he sees Dumbledore as a man who made terrible mistakes.
- Dumbledore admits that his restraint has failed. But he still believes the negotiations are the only way to avoid a civil war inside the castle, so he keeps talking.
- **The stalemate:** The negotiations have become a shield for Voldemort. As long as they continue, he can keep killing Muggle-borns with no response from Hogwarts.
- **The plot:** A faction of the Order led by Moody decides to assassinate Fudge, mainly to derail the negotiations between the Ministry and Hogwarts. If the Minister is killed, the talks can't continue, and Dumbledore can no longer hold Hogwarts back. The stalemate ends, and the embargo has to begin.
- **A secondary benefit:** Without Fudge, Voldemort also loses the public face of his government. For Moody, that's a bonus. The real target is the stalemate.
- **Why it can work:** Kingsley is on Fudge's security detail, and other Order members work in the Ministry and see Fudge every day. Voldemort can't stop them himself, because he can't show up at the Ministry without revealing that he controls it.
- They plan it without Dumbledore. They know he would forbid it, and they've stopped trusting his patience.
- **Harry and Hermione disagree:** They find out about the plot by accident. Harry understands the Order's anger. Hermione warns that killing Fudge could make the Ministry's lies about Hogwarts look true. It's their hardest argument, but because of their promise, they talk it through instead of shutting each other out.
- **The tip-off:** The plan is discussed near a portrait, one of the Ministry's informants. The surveillance system passes the warning straight to Voldemort.

## Part 9: Voldemort's calculation and Dumbledore's death

- **His options:** Voldemort realizes that the stalemate is over either way.
  - If the Order kills Fudge, he loses the Minister he controls and the public face of his government.
  - If he kills Dumbledore, Hogwarts will start the embargo, but Fudge stays alive and in power.
- **His choice:** Killing Dumbledore is the better option. His death gives the Order an outlet, because the embargo becomes their response. That relieves the pressure to kill Fudge. Voldemort decides he can survive an embargo, but not losing the Ministry.
- Draco finishes repairing the Vanishing Cabinet, and his family's lives depend on it working.
- Voldemort and his Death Eaters invade Hogwarts through the Vanishing Cabinet at night. He has to get in and out before anyone outside Hogwarts can prove he was there.
- **Hermione during the invasion:** Harry's first thought is Hermione, but she's already leading the DA to defend the younger students. He trusts her and goes to the tower.
- Voldemort kills Dumbledore on the Astronomy Tower while Harry watches, frozen and unable to help.
- **The plot ends:** With Dumbledore dead, the Order calls off the assassination and turns its anger into the embargo, just as Voldemort predicted.
- **The uprising:** The Slytherin uprising begins. Slughorn leads the loyal Slytherins to stand with McGonagall. It's his finest moment, and it proves that the civil war threat was weaker than everyone feared.
- Draco flees with the Death Eaters, but doesn't fight anyone. Harry notices.
- **The embargo:** McGonagall becomes acting headmistress. She and the Northern Irish council stop all Moonmeal shipments. Hogwarts declares itself independent of the Ministry.
- **The Prophet's version:** Fudge, under the Imperius Curse, claims that Dumbledore was killed by his own reckless followers. He also reveals the Order's plot against him, calling it a plan by "Dumbledore's terrorists." Harry saw Voldemort on the tower, but nobody prints his account.
- **The funeral:** Dumbledore's funeral is held by the lake. Harry knows the next year will be a war, and that Voldemort still wants Hermione. Instead of pushing her away, he takes her hand and tells her that they'll face it together.

## Continuity checklist

- **Voldemort's interest in Hermione:** He learned what she means to Harry through their mind link in Book 5.
- **The capture threat:** Voldemort's order to take Hermione alive continues into Book 7, so he could try again.
- **Snape:** He saves Hermione from capture during the massacre, echoing his guilt over Mary. Harry only learns the truth in Book 7.
- **The Room of Requirement:** Harry and Hermione's private room and Draco's Vanishing Cabinet are both there. The noise they hear behind the wall pays off when Voldemort invades.
- **Hermione's parents:** Her fear for them in Book 6 sets up sending them to Australia in Book 7.
- **Fudge:** He works with Voldemort willingly until the massacre, then is put under the Imperius Curse.
- **Censorship:** It stays hidden in Book 6, then becomes open propaganda in Book 7.
- **Money:** The gold-copying machinery is discovered but not used until Book 7.

## Open decisions

1. **When Harry says "I love you":** I'd make it the night of the massacre. The alternative is Dumbledore's funeral, which is more bittersweet but comes later.
2. **Harry and the plot against Fudge:** I'd keep Harry and Hermione out of it, with Harry sympathizing and Hermione opposing it. The alternative is that Harry joins it, which would make Dumbledore's death partly his fault.
3. **Whether Dumbledore knew:** I'd have Dumbledore find out about the plot just before he dies and choose not to run, knowing his death will finally free Hogwarts to act. The alternative is that he never knows, which is more tragic.
